RBI Master Direction — Credit Risk Management (Commercial Banks)

Applies to commercial banking companies (excluding Small Finance
Banks, Payments Banks, Local Area Banks), corresponding new banks, and
SBI.

Related-Party Lending Restrictions:
Restricts loans to a bank's own directors, their relatives, and
reciprocal lending arrangements (bank A lending to bank B's director
in exchange for bank B lending to bank A's director) — such loans
require specific Board approval and are not routine credit decisions.

Legal Entity Identifier (LEI) Requirement:
Non-individual borrowers with aggregate exposure of Rs. 5 crore or
more must obtain and keep renewing an LEI code — intended to improve
transparency and data accuracy on who a bank is actually exposed to
across the financial system.

CEO / Whole-Time Director Loans:
Loans and advances to a bank's own CEO or Whole-Time Directors require
approval by the Board of Directors, a Board committee with delegated
powers, or the Appointments Committee — not ordinary credit-approval
channels.

Unhedged Foreign Currency Exposure (UFCE):
Banks must assess UFCE risk in borrowers (i.e. borrowers with foreign-
currency-denominated debt or income exposure without hedging), since
exchange-rate volatility can impair a borrower's repayment capacity.
Where UFCE risk is material, banks must apply incremental provisioning
and capital charges reflecting potential losses, and should encourage
borrowers to hedge.

This Direction sets the credit-risk-management framework banks must
have in place — it's a governance/process requirement more than a
single numeric threshold, unlike Capital Adequacy or CRR/SLR.
