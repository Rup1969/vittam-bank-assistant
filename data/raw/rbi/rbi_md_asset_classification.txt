RBI Master Direction — Asset Classification, Provisioning and Income Recognition (Commercial Banks)

IMPORTANT — Two directions exist on this same topic, in force at
different times:
1. The 2025 Direction (issued 28 November 2025) — the current
   applicable rules until the 2026 version takes effect.
2. The 2026 Direction (issued 27 April 2026) — comes into force only
   from 1 April 2027. Until then, the 2025 Direction's NPA
   classification rules remain the operative standard.

2025 Direction — Core Rules (currently in force):
Defines NPA categories (Substandard, Doubtful, Loss assets), security
valuation norms, and "out-of-order" criteria for overdraft/cash-credit
facilities used to determine NPA status. Interest income is recognised
on an accrual basis only for standard (performing) assets; NPAs must
be recognised on a cash basis only (income is booked only when
actually received). All accounts must be covered under an automated,
IT-based system for classification, upgradation, and provisioning —
classification as overdue, SMA (Special Mention Account), or NPA, and
any upgradation, must happen automatically as part of the bank's
day-end processing, not via manual/discretionary flagging.

2026 Direction — What Changes From 1 April 2027:
Introduces a staging framework for asset classification under an
Expected Credit Loss (ECL) approach — a forward-looking provisioning
model — while RETAINING the existing NPA classification norms
alongside it. This is a significant methodology shift (from purely
incurred-loss provisioning to a forward-looking ECL model), so banking
staff should be aware a transition is coming, even though it isn't
live yet.

When answering a question about "current" asset classification rules,
default to the 2025 Direction unless the question specifically asks
about the post-April-2027 ECL framework.
