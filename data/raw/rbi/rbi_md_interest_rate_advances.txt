RBI Master Direction — Interest Rates on Advances (Commercial Banks)

Originally issued in 2016, most recently updated 1 October 2025.

Benchmark Framework:
Banks must price rupee advances (subject to specified exemptions) by
reference to an internal benchmark (Marginal Cost of Funds based
Lending Rate, MCLR) or an External Benchmark Lending Rate (EBLR,
typically tied to the repo rate), plus a Board-approved, bank-defined
spread — negotiating outside this framework is not permitted.

MCLR Composition:
Marginal cost of funds + negative carry on CRR + operating costs + a
tenor premium. MCLR is tenor-linked (published at specified
maturities) and reviewed monthly, or quarterly where permitted.

Reset Periodicity:
For floating-rate loans, the periodicity of interest-rate reset must
be one year or less, and the exact periodicity must be stated in the
loan contract itself — a bank cannot leave this open-ended or apply an
undisclosed reset schedule.

EBLR Adoption Trend (market context, not a rule):
As of December 2024, roughly 61% of total floating-rate loans were
linked to an external benchmark (EBLR), versus about 36% still on
MCLR — RBI has been steering the system toward external benchmarking
for better monetary-policy transmission.

This governs how ANY commercial bank must structure its lending rates
— it's the regulatory basis behind why a bank's home/personal/vehicle
loan rate resets when RBI changes the repo rate.
