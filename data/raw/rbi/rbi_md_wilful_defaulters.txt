RBI Master Direction — Treatment of Wilful Defaulters and Large Defaulters

Purpose: a transparent, non-discriminatory framework for identifying
wilful/large defaulters and sharing that information across the
financial system, so other lenders don't unknowingly extend further
credit to them.

Wilful Defaulter — Definition:
A borrower or guarantor who has committed "wilful default" AND whose
outstanding amount is Rs. 25 lakh or above. Wilful default itself
means: intentional/deliberate misuse, diversion, or siphoning of loan
funds; failing to honour repayment despite having the capacity to pay;
or breaching agreed loan covenants without justification.

Consequences of Being Classified a Wilful Defaulter:
- Barred from further credit from the banking system.
- Restrictions on restructuring existing facilities or starting new
  ventures.
- Reported to Credit Information Companies (monthly).
- Details published (name disclosure).
- Possible criminal proceedings, separate from the credit
  consequences.

Preventive/Ongoing Obligations on Banks:
- Proper credit appraisal before sanctioning.
- End-use monitoring of disbursed funds (checking money is used for
  its stated purpose).
- Involvement of statutory auditors or forensic auditors where
  diversion is suspected.
- Monthly reporting of large and wilful defaulters to Credit
  Information Companies, with accurate reporting of any associated/
  connected parties.

Due Process: classification follows principles of natural justice
(the borrower gets an opportunity to respond) before being formally
declared a wilful defaulter — it is not an automatic/unilateral label.
