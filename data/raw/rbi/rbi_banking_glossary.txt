RBI Banking Glossary — Common Terms

Repo Rate: The rate at which RBI lends short-term funds (typically
overnight) to commercial banks against government securities. RBI's
primary tool for controlling inflation and short-term liquidity.

Reverse Repo Rate: The rate at which RBI borrows funds from commercial
banks, absorbing excess liquidity from the banking system.

Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR): The percentage of a bank's total deposits
that it must keep as reserves with RBI (in cash, not lent out or
invested). Ensures banks retain enough liquidity to meet withdrawal
demand.

Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR): The percentage of a bank's net demand
and time liabilities that must be held in specified liquid assets
(cash, gold, approved government securities). A separate reserve
requirement from CRR, also set by RBI.

Non-Performing Asset (NPA): A loan or advance where the borrower has
not paid interest or principal for a specified period (generally 90
days) — classified as NPA rather than a performing/standard asset,
directly affecting a bank's reported asset quality and profitability.

Marginal Cost of Funds based Lending Rate (MCLR): An internal
benchmark rate a bank uses to price loans, based on its marginal
(incremental) cost of raising funds; many older/legacy loans are still
priced as a spread over MCLR (see sbi_rates_lending_structured for an
example of MCLR-linked loan pricing).

External Benchmark Rate (EBR/EBLR): A lending benchmark tied directly
to an external, RBI-published rate (usually the repo rate) rather than
a bank's own internal cost of funds — used for most new retail and
MSME loans since RBI mandated external benchmarking for these segments.

Bank Rate: The rate at which RBI is ready to buy or rediscount bills of
exchange or other eligible commercial paper — a longer-standing,
less frequently used policy tool than the repo rate.

Marginal Standing Facility (MSF) Rate: The rate at which banks can
borrow overnight funds from RBI against government securities, beyond
what is available under the standard repo window — typically priced
above the repo rate as a penal/emergency-liquidity rate.

This is a general reference glossary, not a substitute for RBI's own
Master Circulars/Directions on any given topic — always confirm the
precise regulatory definition of a term against the specific
Direction/Circular it comes from before citing it as binding.
