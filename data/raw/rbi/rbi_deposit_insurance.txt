Deposit Insurance in India (DICGC)

Bank deposits in India are insured by the Deposit Insurance and Credit
Guarantee Corporation (DICGC), a wholly owned subsidiary of the Reserve
Bank of India, established under the DICGC Act, 1961.

Each depositor in a bank is insured up to a maximum of Rs. 5,00,000
(Rupees Five Lakh) for both principal and interest amount held by them,
across all their deposits in that bank, taken together. This limit
covers savings, fixed, current, and recurring deposit accounts.

This insurance is automatic and free to the depositor — banks pay the
premium to DICGC, not the customer. No application or opt-in is required.

Coverage applies per bank, not per branch — deposits held in the same
capacity and same right across all branches of one bank are added
together and insured up to the Rs. 5,00,000 limit as a whole, not per
branch.

Nearly all commercial banks operating in India, including foreign bank
branches, local area banks, regional rural banks, and cooperative banks,
are covered by DICGC insurance. This limit is set by DICGC/RBI and is
subject to revision by the government from time to time — always confirm
the current limit on dicgc.org.in or rbi.org.in before stating it as final.
