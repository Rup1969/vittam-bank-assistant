RBI Master Direction — Prudential Norms on Capital Adequacy (Commercial Banks)

Minimum CRAR (Capital to Risk-weighted Assets Ratio):
9% of risk-weighted assets at all times — the baseline requirement
before adding the capital conservation buffer below.

Capital Conservation Buffer (CCB):
An additional 2.5% is required on top of the base 9% CRAR, making the
effective minimum CRAR requirement 11.5% in practice.

Tier 1 / Tier 2 Composition (per RBI's parallel Direction for All
India Financial Institutions, which follows the same broad framework —
confirm the exact commercial-bank split against the primary Direction):
- Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1): minimum around 5.5% of risk-weighted
  assets.
- Total Tier 1 capital: minimum around 7%.
- Tier 2 capital: capped at a maximum of 100% of total Tier 1 capital
  for capital-adequacy compliance purposes.

Why This Matters Operationally:
CRAR is the headline solvency metric RBI and the public use to judge a
bank's financial strength — falling below the effective 11.5%
threshold (base + buffer) can trigger supervisory action (see RBI's
Prompt Corrective Action framework, a separate mechanism not detailed
here).

Given related Directions exist for RRBs, AIFIs, and NBFCs with
different specific percentages, always confirm which entity-type
Direction applies before quoting a CRAR figure for a non-commercial-bank
entity.
